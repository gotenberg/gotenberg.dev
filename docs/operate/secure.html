Secure your deployment

Decide what your deployment needs, from who may call Gotenberg to what Gotenberg may reach.

Know the risks​

Gotenberg does what its callers ask: it loads the URLs they name, runs the scripts of the pages it prints, opens their documents and uploads its results where they say. Out of the box, it asks for no credentials and reaches any address its network reaches. Pick the case that matches yours:

  1. Only your backend calls Gotenberg, with content your code controls: keep Gotenberg off the public network. That's enough.
  2. Other services share its network: authenticate the requests, and serve HTTPS.
  3. Requests carry what your users wrote, such as a URL to print, an HTML template or a document: also restrict outbound requests and limit resources.

Authenticate requests​

Basic authentication takes a username and a password from the environment:

compose.yaml
services:
  gotenberg:
    image: gotenberg/gotenberg:9
    environment:
      API_ENABLE_BASIC_AUTH: "true"
      GOTENBERG_API_BASIC_AUTH_USERNAME: gotenberg
      GOTENBERG_API_BASIC_AUTH_PASSWORD: change-me

API_ENABLE_BASIC_AUTH is the variable of the --api-enable-basic-auth flag. The username and password have no flag: set them with these two variables, GOTENBERG_ prefix included.

curl \
  --user gotenberg:change-me \
  --request POST http://localhost:3000/forms/chromium/convert/url \
  --form url=https://example.com \
  -o example.pdf

A request without valid credentials answers 401. /health stays open, so probes need none.

OIDC bearer tokens work instead: set --api-enable-oidc-auth, the issuer with --api-oidc-issuer, and the audience the tokens carry with --api-oidc-audience. Gotenberg finds the signing keys from the issuer's configuration.

Serve HTTPS​

compose.yaml
services:
  gotenberg:
    image: gotenberg/gotenberg:9
    environment:
      API_TLS_CERT_FILE: /certs/cert.pem
      API_TLS_KEY_FILE: /certs/key.pem
    volumes:
      - ./certs:/certs:ro

Gotenberg then serves HTTPS only, on the same port. Kubernetes probes need scheme: HTTPS.

Keep the debug route off​

/debug answers every flag's value. It's off by default: leave --api-enable-debug-route unset where callers you don't trust can reach Gotenberg.

Turn off what you don't use​

  • CHROMIUM_DISABLE_ROUTES, LIBREOFFICE_DISABLE_ROUTES and PDF_DISABLE_ROUTES remove a module's routes.
  • API_DISABLE_DOWNLOAD_FROM turns off downloadFrom, and WEBHOOK_DISABLE turns off webhooks. A webhook sends a request, with headers the caller picks, to any URL the caller names.
  • CHROMIUM_DISABLE_JAVASCRIPT stops the scripts of the pages Chromium prints.

Know the settings that weaken isolation​

Each of these flags trades a protection for a feature. Turn one on only when every caller is trusted:

FlagWhat it gives up
--chromium-proxy-serverThe protection against DNS rebinding, and the outbound checks on the WebSockets a page opens.
--chromium-shared-browser-contextThe isolation of cookies, cache and storage between conversions.
--chromium-disable-web-securityThe same-origin policy in the pages Chromium prints.
--chromium-allow-file-access-from-filesThe barrier between local files a page may read.
--chromium-ignore-certificate-errorsThe TLS checks of the URLs Chromium loads.
--libreoffice-enable-macrosThe barrier against code in documents: macros run inside the container.

The image already runs as an unprivileged user, gotenberg, UID 1001.
