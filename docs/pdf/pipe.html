Run stages

Runs a list of stages on the PDFs you upload, in order.

POST/forms/pdf/pipe
Form data
filesfile[]required

The PDFs, and the files the stages name, such as a stamp's image.

stagesjsonrequired

The stages to run, in order, such as [{"op":"merge"},{"op":"optimize"}].

archivesfile[]

ZIP files Gotenberg unpacks: each entry counts as a file uploaded under files.

downloadFromjson

URLs Gotenberg downloads first, as if you had uploaded the files. Each entry takes a url, and optionally extraHttpHeaders and filename.

Response

The PDF, or a ZIP when several PDFs come out: several uploads without a merge, or a split.

The route also takes the headers. An error answers a status and a message: see Errors.

curl \
  --request POST http://localhost:3000/forms/pdf/pipe \
  --form files=@report.pdf \
  --form 'stages=[
    { "op": "select", "pages": "1-2" },
    { "op": "stamp-text", "text": "DRAFT", "x": 72, "y": 72, "fontSize": 24 },
    { "op": "optimize" }
  ]' \
  -o report.pdf

How stages work​

  • Each stage works on what the previous one produced, so its page numbers count from that PDF.
  • The stages take the PDFs in the alphanumeric order of their names.
  • A stage names the files it needs, such as an image to stamp or a certificate, by their uploaded names. Upload them under files: they don't count as PDFs to process.
  • stages works on this route and on the Chromium and LibreOffice conversions, where the stages run on the converted PDFs. The screenshot routes, the stages' own routes and the read routes answer 400 to it.
  • Each stage also has its own route, /forms/pdf/<op>, which takes its options as form fields, with a list or an object as JSON.

Every stage​

StageWhat it does
mergeAppends the PDFs into one, in the alphanumeric order of their names. The result takes the name of the first.
splitCuts each PDF into several, which come back in a ZIP: report_0.pdf, report_1.pdf… The stages after it run on each part.
selectKeeps the pages you list, in the order you list them.
deleteRemoves the pages you list.
insertInserts the pages of another uploaded PDF.
composeDraws pages of the PDFs on new pages of the size you choose: a label, a packing slip, a sheet of several pages. The result is one new PDF, named after the first.
rotateSets the rotation of pages. Viewers turn them clockwise.
stamp-textDraws a line of text over pages.
stamp-imageDraws an uploaded PNG or JPEG image over pages.
stamp-pdfDraws a page of another uploaded PDF over pages: a letterhead, a seal.
set-metadataWrites the document information. A field you leave out keeps its value.
set-bookmarksReplaces the bookmarks, the outline viewers show beside the pages.
set-formFills form fields.
flatten-formBurns the form fields into the pages, which can then no longer be edited. A value without a stored appearance is drawn, with an installed font for text outside Western European.
flatten-transparencyReplaces transparent content with opaque content, for printers and archival formats that don't support transparency.
convert-colorsConverts every color to one color space.
redactRemoves the content under each region from the file itself, not just from view. Nothing is drawn in its place.
autotagInfers a structure tree for an untagged PDF, so screen readers can follow it. PDF/UA requires one.
attachEmbeds an uploaded file in the PDF.
detachRemoves an embedded file.
extract-attachmentsReturns the embedded files, which come back in a ZIP next to the PDF: invoice_factur-x.xml. /forms/pdf/extract-attachments returns the files alone. Comes last.
extract-textReturns the text the pages show as a .txt file, its pages separated by a form feed, which comes back in a ZIP next to the PDF: report.txt. /forms/pdf/extract-text returns the text alone. Comes last.
optimizeRewrites the PDF to shrink it. Every option is on unless you turn it off. A signed PDF answers 400, and so does an encrypted one, unless the stages decrypt or encrypt it.
encryptProtects the PDF with passwords.
decryptOpens encrypted PDFs and removes their encryption.
remediateRepairs the PDF until it conforms to a profile, or to a PDF/A profile and a PDF/UA one together. Gotenberg-Remediation-Residual counts the violations left: 0 means it conforms.
signSigns the PDF with a certificate you upload.
timestampAdds a document timestamp from an RFC 3161 timestamp authority: proof the PDF existed, unchanged, at that time.
renderDraws pages as images, which come back in a ZIP next to the PDF: report_1.png. /forms/pdf/render returns the images alone.

Order rules​

Gotenberg checks the order of the stages before it runs any, and answers 400 with the rule a list breaks:

  • decrypt comes first, and render, extract-attachments or extract-text last.
  • A list has at most one remediate, one encrypt, one sign and one timestamp. One remediate takes a PDF/A profile and a PDF/UA one together.
  • After remediate, only encrypt, optimize, sign, timestamp, render, extract-attachments and extract-text may follow.
  • After encrypt, only optimize, render, extract-attachments and extract-text may follow.
  • After sign or timestamp, only sign, timestamp, render, extract-attachments and extract-text may follow.
  • encrypt doesn't combine with sign, timestamp, or a PDF/A or Factur-X remediate.
  • decrypt doesn't combine with sign or timestamp: sign the decrypted PDF in a request of its own.

What each stage does to the file​

A stage either rewrites the PDF whole or appends an update to it. A rewrite drops signatures, so on a signed PDF those stages answer 400:

The fileStages
Rewritten, signatures refusedselect, delete, insert, merge, split, compose, encrypt, decrypt, flatten-form, redact, remediate, convert-colors, flatten-transparency, optimize
Updated, signatures keptrotate, stamp-text, stamp-image, stamp-pdf, set-form, set-metadata, set-bookmarks, attach, detach, autotag, sign, timestamp
Unchangedrender, which makes images, extract-attachments, which returns the embedded files, and extract-text, which returns the text

Page selections​

An option of type pages takes a comma-separated list of parts: a page (3), a range (2-5), a range open to the last page (3-) or from the first (-3), or odd, even or last. A ! in front of a part excludes its pages: !1 keeps every page but the first, odd,!1 the odd pages after it, and !last every page but the last. Without an inclusion, the selection starts from every page. Pages count from 1. A page past the end, or a selection that leaves no page, answers 400. all, on its own, names every page.
